The thesis

The market flipped in 2026. Most comparison pages haven't caught up.

Between December 2025 and April 2026, the cheap option moved from compounded drugs to FDA-approved brands sold for cash. Pills arrived. Compounded sellers came under FDA pressure. Buyers now ask different questions, and the page that answers them most honestly will get the click.

It rates brand-name providers only, on six published weighted pillars. That raises the bar for methodology and trust on every comparison page.

Where we can win
  • Show the real cost. Every page quotes a "from $X" price. Nobody shows the all-in monthly cost at the dose people stay on, which is the top complaint in reviews.
  • Route insured and cash buyers separately. Insured, Medicare and cash buyers want different providers. Nobody asks which one you are.
  • Give honest trust signals. Label every option FDA-approved or compounded, date-stamp prices, and flag regulatory issues. Raw Trustpilot stars favour the riskiest sellers.
  • Design for mobile from the start. Stacked cards, filter chips and a sticky button, not a shrunken desktop table.
Decision to make first

Since Oct 29, 2025, Google lets ads mention drug names, but bidding on drug-name keywords (semaglutide, Wegovy, Zepbound, Foundayo) needs Google healthcare certification. LegitScript certifies price-comparison sites. We need to confirm whether HealthAccess qualifies, because it decides which half of the keyword universe we can buy.

How to test it

  • Hero test: drug-led ("Get Wegovy online") vs fear-led ("One all-in price. Cancel anytime. Real people.")
  • Card test: "from $X" vs all-in monthly price as the headline number. Measure click-outs and partner approvals.
  • Stage question in the quiz. Report click-out and partner conversion by stage.
  • Bounce on brand and price ad groups, before vs after the all-in price column.

Avoid: "from $X" as the headline, raw star ratings, "#1 rated", before/after photos, "generic Wegovy" or "same as Ozempic", "guaranteed approval".

Seven findings across 701 reviews

  1. The star rating depends on when it's collected. Each provider asks at its best moment, and none ask at month 3. Invited reviews score 2–2.7★ higher than the rest.
  2. Reps ask for reviews that name them. One SkinnyRx review is the rep's own script: "Please be sure to mention my name."
  3. Each provider breaks at a different stage, and each stage maps to one field in the table.
  4. Prepaid bundles are the biggest money risk. 4 of 6 providers advertise their lowest price on 3–12-month prepay, and refunds are refused when they fail.
  5. The membership surprise comes from the business model: both Ro and Mochi get it.
  6. "Doesn't work" complaints cluster around compounded products, especially oral tablets.
  7. Switching is constant: membership fees push buyers to flat-price providers, failures push them back, and transferred doses are often not honoured.

What this means for HealthAccess

  • Don't show raw Trustpilot stars. They send people to providers that fail in month 2, and the refunds, chargebacks and brand damage come back to us. Show a real customer score that separates invited from unprompted reviews.
  • Measure refill reliability. Every provider gets the first shipment out fast; month 2 is where they fail. No competitor tracks this. It can be a column in the comparison table.
  • Protect people switching providers. Cheap compounded offers pull people away from LillyDirect and other providers, then don't honour their dose. Several went back and paid more.
  • Judge partners on month-2 revenue. If Embody is a partner, ask for refund and chargeback data. Earnings per click should use retained customers, not first sign-ups.
  • Use "felt heard, not judged" in trust copy only for providers that actually deliver it.

Prepay is the hidden risk in the cheapest prices

TrimRx ($1,519 for 6 months) and Embody ($1,428 for 12 months) both show their lowest monthly price on long prepaid bundles. When the provider fails mid-plan, the buyer loses that money and blames whoever sent them. The comparison should show "Pay monthly" vs "Prepay N months" and never use a prepay price as the headline price. Ask partners for chargeback rates by plan length, since these chargebacks land after the payout window.
