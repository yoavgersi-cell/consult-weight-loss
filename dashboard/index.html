The thesis

The market flipped in 2026. Most comparison pages haven't caught up.

Between December 2025 and April 2026, the cheap option moved from compounded drugs to FDA-approved brands sold for cash. Pills arrived. Compounded sellers came under FDA pressure. Buyers now ask different questions, and the page that answers them most honestly will get the click.

Five shifts that changed search intent
Dec 2025 →

Brand drugs got cheap for cash buyers

Zepbound vials $299 / $399 / $449, Wegovy pen $349, Wegovy HD $399. "Cheapest" no longer automatically means compounded.

Jan–Apr 2026

Weight-loss pills launched

Wegovy pill (Jan 5) and Foundayo (Apr 1), both from $149. New searches like "wegovy pill price" and "foundayo vs wegovy pill".

Feb–Jun 2026

FDA cracked down on compounded drugs

Warning letters to 30, then 25 more telehealth firms. A May 2026 proposal would keep sema/tirz off the 503B bulk list. Hims moved to brand drugs.

Jul 2026 →

Medicare started covering GLP-1s

The GLP-1 Bridge charges a flat $50 copay through 2027. At the same time some employers are dropping coverage, so insurance searches go both ways.

Aug 2026

U.S. News published GLP-1 ratings

It rates brand-name providers only, on six published weighted pillars. That raises the bar for methodology and trust on every comparison page.

Where we can win
  • Show the real cost. Every page quotes a "from $X" price. Nobody shows the all-in monthly cost at the dose people stay on, which is the top complaint in reviews.
  • Route insured and cash buyers separately. Insured, Medicare and cash buyers want different providers. Nobody asks which one you are.
  • Give honest trust signals. Label every option FDA-approved or compounded, date-stamp prices, and flag regulatory issues. Raw Trustpilot stars favour the riskiest sellers.
  • Design for mobile from the start. Stacked cards, filter chips and a sticky button, not a shrunken desktop table.
Decision to make first

Since Oct 29, 2025, Google lets ads mention drug names, but bidding on drug-name keywords (semaglutide, Wegovy, Zepbound, Foundayo) needs Google healthcare certification. LegitScript certifies price-comparison sites. We need to confirm whether HealthAccess qualifies, because it decides which half of the keyword universe we can buy.

What they compare on

  1. All-in price at my dose, membership included. "Same price every dose."
  2. Brand vs compounded vs pill, and whether this provider actually has it.
  3. Legitimacy: LegitScript, named US pharmacy, BBB grade, Reddit reputation.
  4. Speed: time to prescription and to the door.
  5. Insurance help: who handles the PA, and what happens if denied.
  6. Cancel and refund terms: cancel online, no long prepay.
  7. A clinician who listens, and support that replies.
  8. Dosing flexibility: microdose, stay low, vials.
  9. Extras: anti-nausea meds, supplies, protein and muscle guidance.
  10. Switching: keep my current dose, no restart at 2.5 mg.

Competitive landscape

Two lanes: editorial sites favour brand-name providers, affiliate landers push compounded

The page has to pick a lane or split them openly, because the people in each lane want different things.

Data & next steps

What we need from HealthAccess to turn this into a plan with numbers

Data request, by value

  1. Google Ads search terms report, last 90 days, with cost and conversions. Shows what users actually look for.
  2. Keyword and ad group structure, with the landing page for each ad group and Quality Score parts.
  3. Partner economics: payout model and amount per partner, and partner-confirmed conversions (earnings per click-out).
  4. On-site funnel by device: landing, scroll, click-out, by list position and CTA type.
  5. Heatmaps and recordings (Hotjar or Clarity), plus past A/B test results.
  6. Full-page screenshots of every landing page variant, desktop and mobile.
  7. Reddit and Trustpilot exports to rebuild the buyer profile from real text.

Open questions for the client

  • Is the site certified (Google healthcare or LegitScript) to bid on drug-name keywords?
  • Which lane: brand-only, compounded, or both shown and clearly split?
  • What conversion is imported into Google Ads: click-out, lead, or partner sale?
  • Do partner contracts forbid bidding on their brand terms?
  • Can partners send back approved and started events, split by device?
  • Who reviews medical claims and compliance copy before a test goes live?
How reliable this research is

Most sources were read through search summaries because the network blocked direct page access, and the client site itself has not been audited yet. Facts are tagged by confidence in the research files. Prices change monthly. Re-verify anything before it goes on a live page.
