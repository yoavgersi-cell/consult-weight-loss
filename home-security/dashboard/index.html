How the industry works

The money is in the monthly fee, not the equipment

Understand this and most of the buyer's pain points make sense. Each block ends with what it means for our page.

1 · How security companies make money, and what the buyer pays

  • The buyer pays two things: the equipment (once, or spread over monthly payments) and the monthly monitoring fee (for as long as they stay). Some brands add an install or activation fee.
  • How equipment is paid differs by route: DIY brands charge it upfront (a starter kit is about $200–350, often on sale). Pro brands either charge it upfront (ADT: $599 minimum with pro install) or finance it over 3–5 years on the monthly bill (Vivint up to 60 months). "Free equipment" means it's folded into a long contract.
  • The company's profit is in the monthly fee (about $54/month on average), paid for years. That's why they discount equipment and lock buyers in: the contract and the cancel fee pay back the discount.
  • Keeping customers is their biggest battle: ADT loses about 13% of customers a year.

For us: buyers need both numbers, equipment and monthly, to know the real cost. Most cards (ours and competitors') show neither, only a gift card or "% off". Showing "equipment + monthly" on every card is a simple way to stand out. And because "free" usually means a contract, buyers who find the contract late feel tricked: 47% of complaints are about getting out.

2 · Two different worlds

  • Pro install (ADT, Vivint, Brinks): a salesperson, a technician visit, often a credit check and a contract. Sold by phone or in the home.
  • DIY (SimpliSafe, Cove, Ring, ADT Blu, Frontpoint): a box in the mail, set up in 30 minutes, cancel anytime. Bought online.
  • The market is moving to DIY: new pro-installed sign-ups hit a 9-year low in 2024, and DIY now outsells pro about 2 to 1.

For us: two buyers need two paths. Pro brands convert by phone, DIY brands by click.

3 · Who actually sells and installs

  • ADT sells directly and through a network of local authorized dealers who sell and install under ADT's name. Many "ADT" ads and phone lines are dealers.
  • Alarm installers must be licensed by the state (for example California's BSIS, Texas DPS). Coverage and install dates differ by area.

For us: this is where the industry is truly local. "Can someone install this near me, and when?" is a real question.

4 · How monitoring works

  • A sensor trips → the monitoring center sees it → they call you → if you don't answer or confirm, they call the police.
  • Many cities require an alarm permit and fine false alarms (a reviewer got a $316 fine from the LAPD). Some cities only send police after the alarm is verified by video, a guard or a second sensor.

For us: buyers judge monitoring in seconds ("called me in 30 seconds"). Response time is a fact worth showing.

5 · How comparison sites make money

  • Per qualified phone call: about $15–40.
  • Per installed system: ADT dealers pay up to about $288.
  • DIY sales: much less, from about $8 to 5–10% of the sale.
  • In home services, about 45% of answered sales calls convert on the call.

For us: this is why every site ranks ADT #1 and pushes the phone. It also means a page that only gets clicks leaves the biggest payouts on the table.

6 · Who buys, and when

  • Moving: nearly 1 in 3 recent buyers moved that year. The #1 trigger.
  • Renters: 37% of recent buyers (8% six years ago).
  • After a break-in or scare, for smart home features (31%), or for an insurance discount.

For us: renters can't sign long contracts or drill walls, so DIY must be easy to find.

Two buying routes, step by step

Pro install ADT, Vivint, Brinks · owners, families, "don't want to deal with it"

  1. Lead: sees the ranking on a comparison site, calls the number or leaves a phone number.
  2. Sales call: a rep calls back within minutes and builds a plan for the home: how many doors and windows, a yard, pets.
  3. Booking: a technician window, e.g. "Tuesday 8am–12pm".
  4. Install day: the technician walks the home and offers add-ons (extra camera, smoke detector), mounts the panel, sensors and cameras.
  5. Handover: sets up the app, shows how to arm and disarm, and the customer signs a 24–36 month monitoring contract.

For our page: the sale happens on the phone and at home, after our click. Our job is a confident, informed caller, so the call and a "get a quote" option matter more than a click.

DIY SimpliSafe, Ring, Cove, Abode · renters, younger buyers, no-contract fans

  1. Build a kit online: base station, keypad, a couple of door sensors, then add a doorbell or motion sensor.
  2. Pay: hardware upfront, then choose free or cheap self-monitoring, or 24/7 pro monitoring at about $20–30/month, no contract.
  3. Delivery: 2–3 business days.
  4. Setup: the app walks them through it. Sensors stick on with adhesive, no drilling.
  5. Done in about 20–45 minutes for a whole home.

For our page: the sale happens on the brand's site in one visit. Our job is to send them in sure of the brand: price, no contract, what's in the kit.

What "a security system" means in the home

  • Hub or control panel: the brain. A wall touchscreen or a box on a shelf, with a siren, backup battery and cellular backup when Wi-Fi or power goes down.
  • Door and window sensors: two small pieces; when they separate, the system knows it opened.
  • Motion and glass-break sensors: in room corners.
  • Video doorbell: see and talk to whoever is at the door; package alerts.
  • Indoor and outdoor cameras: over the garage, the yard, the living room (some indoor ones have a privacy shutter).
  • Smart extras: smart lock, smoke/CO and water sensors, lights.

What happens when the alarm goes off

  1. A sensor trips while the system is armed.
  2. The siren sounds and the app sends an alert.
  3. With pro monitoring, a person at the monitoring center calls the owner in about 15–30 seconds and asks for a safe word.
  4. No answer, or a real break-in: they call local police. False alarm: the owner cancels with the code.

For our page: step 3 is what buyers judge ("called me in 30 seconds" vs "no one called"). Response time is worth showing per brand.

State differences worth knowing

  • Size and crime: Texas, California, Florida and Georgia have the most people and high property-crime suburbs, so the most searches. Logical, not measured by us
  • Police rules: some cities fine false alarms or want a verified alarm (video, guard) before police come. That favours pro monitoring with video check. Confirmed
  • Contracts: ADT is 24 months in California, 36 elsewhere; California's cancel law is strict. Confirmed
  • Climate: battery and solar cameras do worse in freezing northern winters (more wired setups); hot, storm-prone southern states need heat-proof cameras and water sensors. Plausible, no data found

Rules that shape what we can say

  • "Free" claims: the FTC says the catch must sit next to the word "free". A footnote doesn't count.
  • Reviews and counters: the FTC fake-review rule (since Oct 2024) covers made-up reviews and misleading social proof.
  • Auto-renewal: about 30 states have laws; California's is strict (online cancel, clear renewal terms).
  • Door-to-door: 3-day cooling-off right. Vivint paid $20M to the FTC in 2021 over sales practices.
  • ADT trademark: ADT defends its brand hard. Never look like ADT; use the name in ads only for review pages.

Monitoring: who pays for what

  • 19% of homes pay for professional monitoring.
  • 7% pay only for app alerts or video storage.
  • 53% of smart-device owners pay no subscription at all.
  • 60%+ of self-monitoring homes feel just as safe as pro-monitored ones.

How people buy

  • Online is #1: 39% of system owners got theirs online (35% in 2023). Amazon leads for DIY.
  • 51% of shoppers prefer to install it themselves.
  • Only 7% of recent purchases came from local security companies. 74% of dealers lost sales to DIY.
  • Insurance: monitored systems usually earn 5–15% off home insurance.
  • Peaks: Black Friday and Prime Day discounts. Summer burglaries are only ~5–11% above other months.

What this means for us

  • Our page is "systems only". Many visitors want just a camera or doorbell first (the fastest-growing group). A "Cameras & doorbells" version for camera keywords is worth testing.
  • 83% of shoppers want outdoor cameras and 81% a video doorbell in their system. Say on each card if cameras are included.
  • People cross-check on Amazon. Show the real sale price so it matches what they find.

What buyers want, and what they're afraid of

What 423 reviews tell us about the buyer

What they're looking for

  • A real person (37% of reviews, 64% of happy ones). A technician who explains the system, an agent who fixes it on the first call. Bots, long holds and transfers are what make people angry.
  • It just works (16%). Sensors that stay on the wall, cameras that record, an alarm that calls when it goes off.
  • A price they can trust (12%). The number they were told is the number they pay, with nothing added later.
  • Someone responds, fast (7%). Happy reviews quote the time: "within seconds", "called me in 30 seconds".
  • Easy to set up (6%) and cameras that work (9%).

Prices & contracts

Three ways to pay, very different costs to leave

Contract + pro install

ADT, Vivint, Brinks, Xfinity. Low or "free" equipment, but 36–60 months of monitoring or an equipment loan. Leaving early is expensive.

DIY + month-to-month

SimpliSafe, Cove, Ring, Abode, Frontpoint. Buy the kit, install it yourself, cancel anytime. 30–60 day returns.

Cameras + optional plan

Arlo, Wyze, Ring cameras. The plan is mostly video storage. Buyers get annoyed when features need a plan.

Phone & location

Two quick wins for paid mobile traffic, and why

Why the phone matters

  1. Pro install is a big decision. A 3-year contract, a technician in your home, a credit check. People want to ask questions first. In reviews, a real person is the #1 need (37% of all reviews, and 64% of happy ones).
  2. That's how pro brands sell. ADT, Vivint and Brinks run call centers and dealer networks built for phone sales.
  3. That's how they pay. About $15–40 per qualified call, up to about $288 per installed system, compared with much less for a click.
  4. Calls convert. In home services, about 45% of answered sales calls convert on the call.
  5. Our traffic is 100% mobile, where a call is one tap. Top10.com makes the phone the main button on ADT on mobile.

How sure: High on the reasons. The open question is how much it adds versus taking sales from clicks, which is what the test answers.

Why location matters (and where it doesn't)

Home security is a national product sold through local service. The brands and prices are the same everywhere, but installing, police response, contract rules and how people search are local.

What the real reviews change in this plan
