## 1. Market basics

### Size and penetration
- **Market size.** The US home security market is about **$16–18 billion** (2025–2026). MarketDataForecast puts it at $16.16B for 2025 and $17.38B for 2026, growing about 7.5% a year [S1]. Market Research Future gives $16.18B for 2025 [S2]. A 5W PR release says "~$57.8B" [S3], but it clearly counts more (for example, all devices), so do not mix the two figures.
- **How many homes have something.** **48%** of US households own some home security product (a system or a standalone device such as a video doorbell), up from 38% in 2022 (Parks Associates, Q2 2025) [S4]. **14%** of households have devices only (cameras or doorbells, but no full system) [S4].
- **How many have a full system.** **37%** of US internet households own a security *system*, up from 31% in 2022 [S5]. A separate Parks dashboard shows 34% for 2025 [S5]. Use "about one in three homes."
- **Paid service.** **32–35%** of households pay for some security service [S4][S6]. **25%** pay for a service tied to a full system, and **7%** pay for a service tied only to standalone devices [S6].
- **Pro-monitored vs DIY/self.** **19%** of internet households have *professionally monitored* systems. **7%** pay only for non-professional services such as alerts and video storage (Parks, Oct 2025) [S7]. Among paying households, **21%** pay for self-monitoring of video devices [S4].
- **Direction of travel.** New sign-ups for *professionally installed, professionally monitored* systems hit a **nine-year low in 2024** (Parks) [S8]. DIY-wireless now outsells pro-installed systems by about **2:1 by value**, per a marketing firm's estimate [S3]. Self-installation has overtaken pro installation (SafeHome.org 2025 survey) [S9].
- **Average spend.** The average monthly fee for home security services is about **$54/month** (Parks) [S4].

### Who buys
- **Renters are now a big share.** **37%** of people who bought a system in the past year are renters, against **8%** of people who bought more than six years ago (Parks, survey of 8,000 US internet households) [S5]. Camera adoption among renters rose from 42% to 54% in a year (SafeHome.org 2025) [S9].
- **Moving is the #1 trigger.** **Nearly one-third** of households that bought a system in the past year also moved in that year [S5]. Parks calls moving "an important trigger" [S5]. An older Parks analysis ranks "moved into a home where a system was already present" first, with a burglary at the home or a neighbor's home tied for second [S10].
- **Smart-home appeal is rising.** **31%** of 2024 buyers named smart-home features as their main reason, up from 19% in 2021 (Parks) [S8].
- **Ages.** No clean 2025 age split is public. **Nearly half of millennial homeowners** keep full security systems. **56%** of millennial and Gen Z homeowners install safety tech to get insurance discounts, against 37% of all homeowners (Nationwide 2025 survey) [S11].
- **Safety as a reason to move.** **18%** of home hunters say safety or crime is a reason they are moving (homeowners 20.4%, renters 16.3%; Redfin/Ipsos 2024) [S12].
- **New baby.** I found no public 2025–26 number for this trigger. Parks lists "starting a family" among the triggers now ranked below smart-home demand, but gives no share [S8].

## 2. Pricing models, explained simply

**The three basic models**
1. **Contract plus pro install (ADT, Vivint, Brinks, often Xfinity).** You get low or "free" equipment upfront, but you sign a 36–60 month monitoring contract or an equipment loan. Leaving early is costly: ADT charges 75% of the remaining monthly charges [S18], and Vivint requires you to pay off the rest of the equipment loan [S19].
2. **DIY kit plus month-to-month monitoring (SimpliSafe, Cove, Ring, Abode, Frontpoint, Wyze).** You buy the kit (or get it at a discount), install it yourself, and pay monthly with no contract. These brands offer 30–60 day return windows.
3. **Cameras plus optional monitoring (Arlo, Wyze, Ring camera plans, Deep Sentinel).** The subscription is mainly for video storage and AI alerts. Pro monitoring costs extra or comes in a higher tier.

**Words to explain on the page.** Self-monitoring means *you* get the alert on your phone. Pro monitoring means a call center sees the alarm and can send police. "Free equipment" usually means the equipment cost is folded into a contract or loan. Price-lock means your monthly fee will not rise.

## 3. Paid search landscape

### Keyword groups and intent
| Group | Example terms | Intent | Best landing angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic "best" | best home security system, home security companies, top rated | Comparing, early to mid funnel | Ranked list, #1 above the fold |
| Brand | adt, simplisafe, vivint, ring alarm, adt home security | Navigational. Often already decided | Brand review or "X vs alternatives" (trademark rules apply, see below) |
| Brand + deal | adt deals, adt free equipment, simplisafe promo | Ready to buy | Offer-led card, current deal |
| No contract | home security no contract, no monthly fee | Fear of being locked in | Filter to month-to-month brands; show "cost to cancel = $0" |
| Wireless / DIY | wireless home security, diy alarm system | Ready to DIY | DIY brands first |
| Renters | home security for renters, apartment alarm | Renter, no drilling | Renter-safe, no-contract, portable |
| Cameras | home security with cameras, outdoor cameras | Feature-led | Camera plan costs |
| Cheap | cheapest home security, affordable | Price-sensitive | Price-sorted list, 3-year total cost |
| Local | home security near me, alarm company [city] | Wants install or a person | Pro-install brands, click-to-call |
| Free equipment | free home security system | Bargain hunting. Often contract-led | Explain the "free" catch |
| Install | home security installation, professional install | Wants pro install | ADT / Vivint / Brinks with a quote form or call |

### CPCs
- **"home security"**: about 60.5K searches/month at about **$22.30 CPC**. "home security services": about 1.6K searches at about **$33**. "home security cameras": about **$5.94** (Adtargeting.io, date undated) [S35].
- **ADT-branded terms** (iSpionage; country unclear, likely Canada): "adt home security monitoring" $7.73, "adt house security" $9.93, "adt free home security system" **$15.87** [S36].
- **Category benchmarks**: Home & Home Improvement median CPC **$8.33** and median cost per lead **$90.92**. All industries: CPC $5.42, CPL $66.69 (WordStream/LocaliQ 2026) [S37].
- Use the account's own Keyword Planner and Auction Insights data instead of these figures. They are rough third-party estimates.

### Who bids
- **Brands.** ADT (and its authorized dealers, such as SafeStreets), Vivint, SimpliSafe, Brinks and others bid. ADT dealers often run their own lead campaigns [S38].
- **Affiliates and publishers.** SafeWise runs many **paid-only landing pages** under `/lp/` URLs, each with its own angle: "Best Home Security Systems of 2026", "Best Home Security Providers", "Best Security Systems with Cameras", "Best Security Companies Near Me" (`/best-home-security-system/lp-3/`), "Best Home Security Reviews", and a social version [S39]. **The #1 pick changes by landing page.** One SafeWise page puts SimpliSafe first, another puts ADT and Vivint first for pro install [S39][S40]. This is strong evidence that the big affiliates **match the #1 pick to the keyword's intent**.
- Security.org, SafeHome.org, US News 360, Forbes Home, CNET, Reviews.org, This Old House, Top10.com, Top Consumer Reviews and Best Company all publish "best" lists. I could not confirm from this session which of them bid on Google or Bing. Check Auction Insights and the Ads Transparency Center.
- **Copycat or thin sites** (for example `*.pages.dev` sites) use banners like "Our #1 Pick for 2026: SimpliSafe — Get 40% OFF This Month GET DEAL" [S41]. That is the low end of the market we compete against.

### Ad copy patterns (from page titles and offer pages; check live ads)
- **Year and "best"**: "Best Home Security Systems of 2026", "7 Best…", "Top 10…" [S39][S40].
- **Authority**: "Reviewed and Tested by Experts", "hundreds of hours of tests" [S40][S42].
- **Offer-led**: "50% off any new system + free outdoor camera" (SimpliSafe) [S23]; "Free Nest Doorbell + $100 Visa gift card" (ADT dealer SafeStreets, with a $99 install and a monitoring contract) [S23]; "up to 60% off equipment" (ADT) [S23].
- **Fear removal**: "no contract", "cancel anytime", "60-day money-back".
- **Local**: "near me", city names.

### Rules on brand bidding
- **Google Ads.** Bidding on a competitor's trademark *as a keyword* is generally allowed. Google restricts trademarks in **ad text**. A **reseller** or **informational site** may use a trademark in ad text if the landing page is mainly about that trademarked product, and the ad and page make clear what kind of site it is. Otherwise you need the owner's authorization [S43].
- **Microsoft Advertising (Bing).** Trademark keywords have been allowed since 2011. Ad text is policed on complaint. Resellers and **informational sites such as product reviews** are allowed uses [S44].
- **ADT's history.** ADT defends its brand hard in court, though I found no case about keyword bidding. Cases include ADT v. Vivint (salespeople posing as ADT; Vivint paid **$10M** in 2017) [S45]; ADT's suit against a call center that "masquerades as ADT" [S45]; and ADT v. Ring over the blue octagon sign design [S45]. **Practical rule:** never imply you *are* ADT, never use ADT's logo or blue octagon, and if you use "ADT" in ad text, send the click to a page that is mainly about ADT and clearly labeled as a review.
- **Local-services policy.** Locksmiths need advanced verification in the US [S46]. Home security is not on Google's restricted list in the sources found, but a reseller guide says it often triggers extra policy review [S46].

**Lead-gen vs click-out vs call**
- **Click-out (DIY brands).** The visitor buys on the brand's site, and you earn a CPA or a percentage. Pay is lower per sale, but the funnel is simple. Best fit for SimpliSafe, Cove, Ring, Abode and Wyze.
- **Calls (pro brands).** Calls convert well. In home services, **38%** of answered calls are leads and **45%** of those convert on the call (Invoca 2026). Its 2025 benchmark puts home services at **46%** conversion, the top vertical [S49]. Do *not* cite the old "calls convert 10–15x better" claim, which cannot be traced to a real study [S49].
- **Lead forms ("Get a quote").** These earn a CPL ($15+) or a CPA once installed ($125–$288). Lead quality rules (call length, home owned, credit) decide whether you get paid.
- **Sum-up.** Pro brands pay the most per conversion, and they want calls. DIY brands pay less, but mobile users finish the purchase more easily. A good page offers both.

## 6. Rules and risks

- **Click-to-cancel / negative option.** The FTC's 2024 Click-to-Cancel rule was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025** (for procedural reasons) and never took effect [S52]. The FTC reopened rulemaking with an **ANPRM on 11 March 2026** [S53]. ROSCA (the online negative-option law) and the old Negative Option Rule still apply, and the FTC keeps enforcing them [S52][S53]. For us: if a card shows a monthly plan, state that it renews and how to cancel.
- **State auto-renewal laws.** About **30 states** have them, and some are stricter than the vacated federal rule [S52]. **California AB 2863** (in force from 1 July 2025) requires online click-to-cancel and express consent to renewal terms, and covers free-to-paid conversions [S54].
- **"Free" claims.** Under the FTC "Free" Guide (16 CFR 251), all conditions for getting and keeping the free item must be shown **clearly and conspicuously at the outset**. An asterisk pointing to a footnote **does not count** [S55]. "Free equipment" that needs a 36-month contract must say so next to the word "free."
- **Fake reviews rule.** In force since **21 October 2024**. It bans fake or AI-written reviews, bought reviews and undisclosed insider reviews. Penalties are up to about **$53,088 per violation** (2025 inflation figure). The FTC sent its first warning letters to 10 companies on **22 December 2025** [S56]. For us: any star rating or quote on a card must be real and sourced, and our scores must be labeled as *our* editorial ratings.
- **Door-to-door sales.** Vivint paid **$20M** (a $15M civil penalty plus $5M for consumers) in 2021 to settle FTC charges that sales reps misused credit reports, including "white paging" [S57]. Vivint also settled with states over door-to-door tactics, including Wyoming ($100K), Missouri, Kansas ($55K), Texas and Pennsylvania (reps claiming to be the customer's existing provider) [S58]. In 2017 Vivint paid ADT $10M over reps posing as ADT [S45]. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule gives 3 days to cancel door-to-door sales [S19].
- **Trademark disputes.** ADT v. Vivint (2017, $10M; 2020 suit) [S45]. ADT v. Ring over the blue octagon [S45]. "Brinks Home" is a **licensed** name: Monitronics pays royalties to The Brink's Company [S17]. Do not mix up Brinks Home with Brink's or present it as related to ADT. I found no Brink's v. Monitronics trademark lawsuit.
- **Brand risk signals for card copy.** ADT attrition rose to 13.1% [S13]. AAA reportedly stopped accepting Brinks Home disputes [S17]. Brinks has a 1.2-star Trustpilot score according to one review site [S22]. Stick to facts on the cards and do not make up claims.

## 7. Ten implications for a mobile comparison page that converts paid search

1. **Match the #1 card to the keyword.** SafeWise runs many `/lp/` pages, and their #1 pick changes (SimpliSafe on DIY pages, ADT or Vivint on pro-install pages) [S39][S40]. Build at least three variants: generic/pro (ADT #1), no-contract/DIY/renters (SimpliSafe or Cove #1), and cheap (Ring or Cove #1).
2. **Lead with a "move-in" angle.** Moving is the top trigger, and nearly a third of recent buyers moved that year [S5]. Use a hero like "Just moved? Compare security in 60 seconds" and add a "Moving" filter or quiz answer.
3. **Make renters a first-class path.** 37% of recent buyers rent [S5]. Add a "I rent" toggle that shows no-drill, no-contract and take-it-with-you brands.
4. **Show cost to leave, not just monthly price.** ETFs range from $0 (SimpliSafe, Cove) to 75% of the remaining term (ADT, Brinks) and $1,200 (Deep Sentinel) [S18][S22][S34]. A "Contract: none / 36 mo" chip plus a "Cancel fee" line answers the biggest fear (TRAPPED_CONTRACT in our codebook).
5. **Show a 3-year total.** Prices range from $19.99/mo (Ring Pro) to $49.99+ (ADT, Frontpoint) [S20][S26][S30], and equipment is often financed. A single "3-yr est. total" number lets mobile users compare without opening a table.
6. **Put the catch next to every "free" claim.** The FTC Free Guide says footnotes don't count [S55]. Write "Free doorbell with $99 install + monitoring contract" on one line [S23]. This keeps us compliant and builds trust.
7. **Two CTAs for pro brands: Call and Get quote.** Pro brands pay per qualified call or install ($15–$40 per call, up to $288 per install) [S38][S50], and home-services calls convert at about 45% [S49]. On mobile, a tap-to-call button with hours ("Agents available now") should sit beside "Get quote." DIY brands keep a single "See deal" click-out.
8. **Use trust signals the trial windows back up.** SimpliSafe and Cove offer 60-day returns [S25][S28], and Cove locks the rate on Plus [S28]. Show "60-day money-back" and "Price-lock" as badges. They are factual, and they counter the PRICE_HIKE and CANCEL_HARD fears.
9. **Keep brand-term traffic safe.** You may bid on "ADT" and "SimpliSafe" keywords, but brand use in ad text needs an informational or review landing page about that brand [S43][S44]. Send brand clicks to a single-brand review page with alternatives, never the generic list. Never look like the brand: no logo lookalikes or blue octagon [S45].
10. **Keep scores honest and explained.** The fake-reviews rule is now being enforced [S56], and security.org-style 9.8/10 scores with "Best for" badges are the norm [S42]. Use a short "How we score" link, label scores as editorial, disclose affiliate pay near the top, and only show user ratings with a source and date.
