# 04 — US Home Security: What People Buy and Who Buys

*Research date: 2 November 2026. Every number has a source link. Where data is missing, old, or the sources disagree, this file says so.*

**How to read this file**
- **"Internet households"**: Parks Associates, the main industry tracker, measures US households *with internet*. That covers most homes, but not all. Its numbers are not the same as "all US households."
- Survey numbers from SafeHome.org, Security.org, Cove, SimpliSafe and similar sites come from **vendor or affiliate surveys**. They are useful, but they are not neutral research.
- Prices change every week because of promotions. Treat every price as a rough guide, not a quote.

### 1b. What is the most common entry product: video doorbell or camera?

- **No source says directly which device people buy first.** Parks tracks "purchase triggers," but that data sits in paid reports.
- **Best evidence:** **cameras** are owned by more households (33%) than **video doorbells** (20–27%) ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-19-of-internet-households-have-professionally-monitored-systems-while-7-pay-for-non-professional-services-like-alerts-and-video-storage-302596302.html)). Over one-third of recent video doorbell buyers were first-time doorbell buyers ([Parks 2021, old](https://www.securityinfowatch.com/residential-technologies/smart-home/press-release/55036196/parks-associates-parks-associates-30-of-internet-households-own-either-a-smart-camera-or-a-smart-video-doorbell)).
- The "device only" group (camera or doorbell, no system) grew from **7% to 12%** of households from 2022 to 2026 ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)). This shows that a standalone video device is a very common first step.
- **Shopper demand:** 83% of shoppers want outdoor cameras in their system, and 81% want a video doorbell ([Security.org 2026](https://www.security.org/resources/diy-home-security-study/)).
- **Working answer:** cameras and doorbells are close. Cameras are slightly ahead in ownership. The doorbell is a cheap, simple "gateway" product (for example, Ring sells alarm kits bundled with a doorbell, see 1d). Treat "doorbell first" as a hypothesis, not a fact.

### 1e. All-in-one kits vs building piece by piece

- Shoppers lean DIY: **51%** prefer to install it themselves, and **74%** of those with a pro install have thought about switching. The top reason (43%) is being able to install on their own schedule ([Security.org 2026](https://www.security.org/resources/diy-home-security-study/)).
- About **49%** of people with alarm systems installed them themselves (SafeHome, cited by [Security.org](https://www.security.org/resources/diy-home-security-study/)).
- **Gap:** no source splits "bought a bundled kit" vs "added devices one at a time." The fast growth of device-only households (7% to 12%) suggests many people build up step by step ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)).

### 1f. Professional monitoring vs self-monitoring vs cloud video plans

- **19%** of internet households have professional monitoring. **7%** pay for non-professional services only, such as alerts or video storage ([Parks, Oct 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-19-of-internet-households-have-professionally-monitored-systems-while-7-pay-for-non-professional-services-like-alerts-and-video-storage-302596302.html)).
- **53%** of smart home device owners pay **no** subscription. **19%** pay for a video device service ([Parks 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-19-of-smart-home-device-owners-pay-for-subscription-service-for-a-video-security-device-adoption-of-smart-home-hubs-remains-flat-at-5-of-us-internet-households-302861262.html)).
- **60%+** of self-monitoring households say DIY keeps them as safe as pro monitoring ([Parks 2024](https://www.parksassociates.com/blogs/energy-pr/parks-associates-consumer-study-demand-for-security-solutions-devices-systems-and-services-shows-60-of-us-households-with-self-monitoring-report-feeling-that-diy-keeps-them-as-safe-as-professional-monitoring)).

### 1g. What is growing and what is declining

**Growing**
- Systems overall: 31% → 37% of internet households, 2022 to 2026 ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)).
- Camera or doorbell with no system: 7% → 12% ([same](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)).
- DIY and online buying: online buying rose from 35% to 39%. **74%** of dealers have lost sales to DIY or standalone devices, up from 51% in 2023 ([Parks](https://www.securityinfowatch.com/residential-technologies/smart-home/news/55369245/parks-associates-parks-associates-reports-sharp-rise-in-dealer-losses-to-diy-security)).
- Even ADT is pushing DIY: its DIY subscriber additions were up nearly 23% ([ADT Q4 2025 call](https://www.aol.com/finance/adt-adt-q4-2025-earnings-163438600.html)).
- AI features: 28% of device users already have AI person or package detection ([SafeHome 2026, via secondary write-up](https://www.safehome.org/resources/home-security-industry-annual/)).
- Smart locks: slow, steady growth to ~11% ([Parks](https://www.parksassociates.com/blogs/in-the-news/the-smart-money-the-evolution-of-residential-access-control)).

**Flat or declining**
- Traditional local dealers: only **7%** of recent system purchases came from local security companies ([Parks at ISC West](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-at-isc-west-among-recent-buyers-of-security-systems-only-7-of-purchases-are-coming-from-local-home-security-companies-302112794.html)).
- Pro-monitoring base: ADT had about **6.3 million** monitored subscribers (Sept 2025), which looks roughly flat ([ADT 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305625000166/adt-20250930.htm)).
- Smart home hubs: flat at **5%** ([Parks 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-19-of-smart-home-device-owners-pay-for-subscription-service-for-a-video-security-device-adoption-of-smart-home-hubs-remains-flat-at-5-of-us-internet-households-302861262.html)).
- **Gap:** no current data found on landline monitoring decline.

### 2b. Typical spend

- **Upfront:** DIY starter kits are about **$200–$350** list, often less on sale (see 1d). A whole-home DIY setup is about **$435–$675** (Cove example). A pro system is up to **$749.99** upfront (Vivint).
- **Monthly:** DIY pro monitoring is about **$20–$33** (SimpliSafe $22.99–$32.99; Cove $19.99–$29.99; Ring ~$19.99). Pro brands charge about **$29–$60** (ADT $28.99–$59.99; Vivint ~$47). Self-monitoring or video storage costs about **$4–$10**.
- About **25%** of new ADT direct subscribers now buy equipment outright instead of financing it into a contract ([ADT Q4 2025](https://www.aol.com/finance/adt-adt-q4-2025-earnings-163438600.html)).
- **Gap:** no national average monthly bill from a neutral source.

### 2c. How long people research, and how many brands they compare

- **No data found** on how long people research or how many brands they compare. Security.org's sample was shoppers planning to buy *within 12 months* ([Security.org](https://www.security.org/resources/diy-home-security-study/)).
- Brand awareness is concentrated. Ring and ADT have "commanding leads" when people name their main brand ([SafeHome 2026](https://www.safehome.org/resources/home-security-industry-annual/)). ADT leads among homeowners with alarm systems, and Ring leads among renters ([SafeHome](https://www.safehome.org/resources/home-security-industry-annual/)). ADT ranked "most trusted" in Lifestory Research's 2024 study, which was based on 4,413 opinions ([Lifestory](https://www.lifestoryresearch.com/2024-best-security-system-ranking-review)).

### 2d. Seasonality

- **Burglary peaks are smaller than people think.** In older BJS data, winter burglaries were 26% lower than summer. In more recent data, the gap is only 11% ([BJS](https://bjs.ojp.gov/content/pub/pdf/spcvt.pdf)). FBI NIBRS data for 2022–2024 suggests summer is only about **5.6%** above the rest of the year ([MoneyGeek / secondary](https://www.moneygeek.com/living/home/summer-burglary-by-region/)). July and August are still the commonly cited peak months ([Frontpoint, citing FBI 2010](https://www.frontpointsecurity.com/blog/fbi-states-july-and-august-worst-burglary-months)).
- **Holidays / Black Friday:** brands run their biggest discounts, sometimes up to 60% off or free equipment ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/best-black-friday-deals-home-security-medical-alerts-systems/)). Prime Day also drives deep Ring discounts ([Security.org](https://security.org/home-security-systems/ring-alarm/prime-day/)). **No source measures the sales spike.**
- **Moving season:** no direct data. The indirect sign is that 37% of recent system buyers are renters, who move often ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)).

## 3. Typical buyers (personas)

**Demographic notes:**
- Younger adults own more security devices. Kastle reports 45% of Millennials have a system vs 12% of Baby Boomers ([Kastle](https://info.security.kastle.com/resources/articles/burglary-home-security-statistics)). Statista found 38% of people aged 18–29 own smart safety devices vs 37% of those aged 30–49 ([Statista, Sept 2024](https://www.statista.com/statistics/228880/people-living-in-households-that-own-a-home-security-system-usa/)).
- Men are more likely than women to own a system: 64% vs 46% ([Cove 2023](https://www.covesmart.com/resources/diy-home-security/the-security-gap-why-don-t-people-have-a-home-security-system/)).
- **Income:** no national breakdown found. A New Jersey survey found income does not reliably predict ownership ([BGS 2026](https://www.bgs.us/resources/survey-one-in-three-nj-homeowners-no-security-system/)).
- **Urban vs suburban:** no data found.

### Persona 1: Homeowner family (the core buyer)
- **Evidence:** 63% of households with kids own a smart home product, vs 37% without kids ([Parks 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-estimates-approximately-13-million-new-us-internet-households-have-entered-the-smart-home-market-since-2020-302523800.html)). Only 21% of homeowners have no security system ([SafeHome](https://safehome.org/resources/home-security-industry-annual)).
- **Who:** ages 30–49, owns a house, often suburban (suburban is an assumption; no data found).
- **Motivations:** protecting kids, packages and the home. Insurance discount.
- **Buys:** a full system plus outdoor cameras and a doorbell, often with pro monitoring.
- **Worries:** monthly cost, contracts, false alarms.
- **Fits:** ADT, SimpliSafe, Vivint (pro install, full coverage).

### Persona 2: Renter / new mover
- **Evidence:** 37% of recent system buyers rent ([Parks 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-37-of-recent-home-security-system-buyers-are-renters-as-apple-enters-the-smart-home-market-302914107.html)). Renters with no security fell from 36% to 30%, and renter camera use rose from 42% to 54% in a year ([SafeHome](https://safehome.org/resources/home-security-industry-annual)). Of renters without cameras, 42% say their landlord doesn't allow them (survey surfaced alongside [Cove](https://www.covesmart.com/resources/diy-home-security/the-security-gap-why-don-t-people-have-a-home-security-system/) and [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/mortgages/studies/2025-cost-of-home-safety-report) results; exact original source unconfirmed).
- **Who:** younger, apartments, more urban (urban is an assumption).
- **Motivations:** package theft, feeling safe in a new place.
- **Buys:** indoor cameras, a battery doorbell, small DIY kits with no drilling.
- **Worries:** landlord rules, contracts, taking it with them when they move.
- **Fits:** Ring, SimpliSafe, Cove, Wyze (no contract, portable).

### Persona 3: Older adult / aging in place
- **Evidence:** 30% of adults 50+ use home security tech that monitors home safety, and 38% have a device that shows who is at the door ([AARP 2026 via Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jamiegold/2026/04/07/5-trends-from-aarp-report-show-how-older-adults-are-using-tech-at-home/)). 64% of adults 50+ are open to a medical emergency response system, and 44% to doorbell or security cameras ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/livable-communities/aarp-research-insights-livable-communities/)). Only 12% of Boomers have a system ([Kastle](https://info.security.kastle.com/resources/articles/burglary-home-security-statistics)).
- **Who:** 50+, mostly homeowners.
- **Motivations:** emergencies, falls, fire, scams at the door. Adult children want to check in.
- **Buys:** a monitored system with smoke/CO, a doorbell camera, and sometimes a medical alert.
- **Worries:** setup difficulty, data privacy, cost on a fixed income.
- **Fits:** ADT (pro install, medical add-ons), SimpliSafe, Cove.

### Persona 4: Post-break-in buyer (urgent)
- **Evidence:** in one vendor survey, 78% of burglary victims bought a system afterward (1,200-adult survey, Aug 2025, found via [ConsumerAffairs 2026](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/home-security-statistics.html); vendor-published, small sample, exact page unconfirmed). Half of homeowners who had a security issue invested in a system ([Insurify](https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/insights/home-security-statistics/)).
- **Who:** any age. Searches with high urgency.
- **Motivations:** fear, wanting fast help and proof for police and insurers.
- **Buys:** a full system with pro monitoring, outdoor cameras and a siren.
- **Worries:** how fast they can get it installed, whether someone will actually respond.
- **Fits:** ADT and Vivint (pro install), SimpliSafe (ships fast, active guard plans).

### Persona 5: Smart-home enthusiast
- **Evidence:** 18% of US households have 6 or more smart devices ([Parks](https://www.parksassociates.com/blogs/pr-smart-home/parks-associates-18-of-us-households-have-six-or-more-smart-home-devices)). 51% of recent smart home buyers bought 3 or more devices in a year ([Parks](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parks-associates-51-of-recent-smart-home-device-buyers-bought-three-or-more-devices-in-the-past-year-301865245.html)).
- **Who:** younger, tech-comfortable, often male (men own systems more, per Cove).
- **Motivations:** control, automation, works with Alexa, Google or Apple. AI detection.
- **Buys:** piece by piece: cameras, locks, sensors, often self-monitored.
- **Worries:** subscription creep, lock-in to one ecosystem, privacy and hacking.
- **Fits:** Ring, Wyze, abode, Eufy, SimpliSafe.

### Persona 6: Small landlord
- **No reliable data found** on small landlords buying security.
- Related facts: 54% of renters said they would pay higher rent for a connected security device or doorbell ([Insurance Journal, 2019, old](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2019/06/28/530725.htm)). 71% of apartment renters want management to do more on security ([Deep Sentinel 2024, vendor](https://www.securitysales.com/news/71-u-s-apartment-renters-more-security-property-managers/162769/)).
- **Likely buys:** doorbells, smart locks (for tenant turnover), outdoor cameras.
- **Fits:** Ring, Wyze, smart lock brands. Treat this persona as a hypothesis.

## 5. Five takeaways for a mobile paid-search comparison page that ranks providers

1. **Lead with "no contract + true monthly price."** Cost (33–36%) and "don't need it" (35%) are the top barriers, and cost drives cancellations. Show each provider's **upfront price, monthly price and contract length** in the first screen of each ranking card. DIY monitoring is about $20–$33/mo, pro brands about $29–$60/mo, and ADT's 36-month contract is a real difference.
2. **Segment above the fold: "Own" vs "Rent" (and "Just a camera/doorbell" vs "Full system").** 37% of recent buyers are renters, and device-only households nearly doubled (7% to 12%). A one-tap filter lets renters jump to Ring, SimpliSafe and Cove, and homeowners to ADT, Vivint and SimpliSafe.
3. **Sell DIY and online, but keep pro install as an option.** 51% of shoppers prefer DIY, and online is now the #1 channel (39%), with Amazon the top shopping place. Badges like "installs in 30–60 minutes" and "pro install available ($129.99 Cove)" answer both groups. Expect people to cross-check prices on Amazon, so show sale prices that match.
4. **Make cameras and doorbells the hero visuals.** 83% want outdoor cameras and 81% want a video doorbell. Show what's in each starter kit (hub, keypad, sensors, *camera?*) so people can compare like with like. Kits differ a lot (Ring's 5-piece has 1 contact sensor; SimpliSafe Core has 3 plus a camera).
5. **Answer trust and privacy in one line per brand, and time spend to deals.** Privacy worries are high (62%), so add short facts per brand (local video storage, encryption, who can see the footage). Ring and ADT dominate awareness, so the page must explain *why* a challenger ranks higher. Plan bigger budgets and "deal" messaging for Prime Day and Black Friday. Summer burglary peaks are real but small (about 5–11%), so don't over-weight them.
