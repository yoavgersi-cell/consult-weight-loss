## A rep's review-request script, posted as a review

One "review", from an invited account, is the rep's own message pasted in:

> "It's my pleasure talking to you today! If you could take a moment to share a brief review about the assistance I provided, I would greatly appreciate it. Please be sure to mention my name, '[Carmy or Carmelyn]', in your feedback."

This shows that **reps ask customers directly for reviews that name them**, most likely because they're scored on it. That explains why SkinnyRx's ratings are high and so focused on reps. Asking for reviews is allowed. It does mean the score measures the phone call, not the treatment. Reviews without the "Invited" label are mostly rep shout-outs too, which suggests reps also ask outside Trustpilot's system.

## What this means for HealthAccess

1. **Label oral compounded GLP-1s clearly.** SkinnyRx sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide as tablets and dissolving tablets. No oral tirzepatide is FDA-approved, and the approved oral options (Wegovy pill, Foundayo) are different products. The comparison should show **"Compounded oral — not FDA-approved, effectiveness not established"** on these, separately from the Wegovy pill and Foundayo. Otherwise needle-averse first-timers searching "weight loss pill" will land on an option with the weakest evidence. *[Confirm the current regulatory wording with the client's compliance reviewer.]*
2. **Prepaid bundles fail again: this is now the fourth provider** (TrimRx, Embody, WellMedr, SkinnyRx). SkinnyRx's bundles run up to $3,588 for 12 months with "no refunds on bundles". Pay-monthly pricing should be the default, and every prepay offer needs its refund terms shown next to it.
3. **Checkout defaulting to a bigger bundle** ("reverts your order to what is recommended") is a consumer-protection risk. If it's true, it is the kind of practice that could reflect on a site that sent the buyer there.
4. **A score built mostly on rep shout-outs says nothing about results.** Combined with the script, this is the clearest case yet for a real customer score that ignores reviews about a rep interaction and counts results and reliability.
