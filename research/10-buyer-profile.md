# 10 — The online weight-loss buyer: profile and site messaging

**Built from:**
- 701 hand-coded Trustpilot reviews across 6 providers (Ro, Mochi, WellMedr, Embody, TrimRx, SkinnyRx) — files 04–09
- the earlier public research — files 01–03

Reddit data will be added next using the same codebook (`research/CODEBOOK.md`).
**Status:** v1, Oct 7, 2026. Numbers are from the review sample. They are directional, not a survey.

## 1. Profile of the typical buyer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | An adult paying cash, most often aged 40–64 (KFF: 22% of 50–64-year-olds use a GLP-1). Reviews range from "single mother of two" to "about to retire" to "a 72-year-old man". One provider turned away a 75-year-old (age cap 74). Older buyers are a real share of the market, not an edge case. |
| **How they pay** | Mostly out of pocket. Insurance shows up as a hope ("they ran it through my insurance and it was approved the same day") or a reason they came online ("my primary care doctor… was not able to get me approved"). |
| **History** | Has tried diets for years: "For years I kept trying to lose weight and nothing stuck." Often has used a GLP-1 before, through another provider, a doctor or LillyDirect. |
| **State of mind** | Hopeful but wary. "I went in skeptical because the price looked low." "I was really nervous about doing this journey online." They expect to be overcharged, and many have been before. |
| **What they choose on** | Price: one monthly number, no surprises. |
| **What they rate on** | **People and reliability**: a human who answers, a clinician who listens, medication arriving on time. |
| **Why they leave** | Practical problems, not medical ones: late refills, surprise charges, no reply from support, cancellations ignored. |

**In one line:** *they shop on price, stay for the people, and leave when the medication or the bill goes wrong.*

## 2. Where buyers are in their journey

Of the 81 reviews that say where the writer is in their journey:

| Stage | Share | What they say | What they need from a comparison page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starting out** (first GLP-1, or first time online) | **43%** | "my first time using a weight loss drug" · "nervous about… giving myself the injection" · "on the fence… finally take the plunge" | Reassurance, how it works, what month 1 vs month 4 costs, a real clinician, no judgment |
| **Switching providers** | **32%** | "My last provider charged about twice as much" · "The old provider stopped answering" · "I switched from Lilly Direct because the cost was prohibitive" | Keep my dose, lower all-in price, someone who answers, fast first shipment |
| **Established (3+ months)** | **25%** | "Six months in, I am down 24 pounds" · "with them for two years" · "5 lbs away from my goal" | Price at higher doses, reliable refills, flexibility (pause, maintenance), easy cancellation |

**Caveats:**
- Trustpilot over-represents new sign-ups, because companies invite reviews at checkout and after the consult.
- Reddit will over-represent experienced users.
- The true split for HealthAccess's own traffic should come from one quiz question: **"Have you taken a GLP-1 before?"** (No · Yes, with another provider · Yes, 3+ months · Yes, but I stopped).

**Switching is constant, and it runs in both directions:**
- Membership fees push people to flat-price providers.
- Failures push them back to brand-name or bigger providers (back to Ivim, LillyDirect, Ro, "a real MD").
- At least 6 reviews say the new provider didn't honour their current dose.

## 3. What they look for, ranked

Themes from 701 reviews, counting 4–5★ (praise) and 1–2★ (complaints).

| # | Need | Praise | Complaints | In their words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A real human who answers** | 201 | 70 | "I NEVER talked to a bot" · "a live person to talk to" · named reps everywhere |
| 2 | **An easy, fast start** | 161 | 0 | "easy and quick" · "approved the same day" · "meds at my door in 2 days" |
| 3 | **Being heard, not judged** | 121 | 16 | "I felt seen. Not judged." · "did not make me feel ashamed of my slow progress" |
| 4 | **Medication on time, every month** | 51 | **81** | "First month is good, after that good luck" · "missed 2 doses" |
| 5 | **Control over their money** (cancel, refunds, no surprise charges) | 12 | **65** | "charged me again after I cancelled" · "no refunds on bundles" |
| 6 | **A price they understand** (one all-in number) | 44 | 30 | "Price shown was what I paid, no weird add-ons" · "It said $39 then charged me over $200" |
| 7 | **That it works** | 34 | 31 | Results are rare in praise; "doesn't work" clusters around compounded oral and "watered down" products |
| 8 | **Dose continuity / medication type** | 21 | 20 | "will NOT honor… if you are already at a certain dose" · "I know it's FDA approved" |

**What the ranking shows:**
- Delighters (people, easy start, being heard) create praise.
- Basics (on-time medication, control of money) create complaints.
- A comparison page can't show delighters credibly, since every provider claims them. It *can* show the basics before the click: billing, cancellation, prepay terms and refill reliability. That's where HealthAccess can stand out.

## 4. What buyers fear before they click

1. **Being overcharged:** hidden membership fees, a starter price that doesn't last, billing every 21 days.
2. **Being trapped:** prepaid bundles with no refund, cancellations that are ignored, two subscriptions to cancel.
3. **Running out of medication:** late refills and missed doses, especially during a month-2 refill or a promotion.
4. **Being scammed:** "Is this a scam?", or medication that "doesn't work".
5. **Nobody to call:** bots, a different agent every time, "a supervisor will call you" and no one does.
6. **Being judged:** about weight or slow progress. The sales call that "read my weight from the intake form" is what this sounds like.

## 5. What this means for HealthAccess's site

### A. Headline and the bullets under it
Lead with what buyers fear, not with the drug.
- **Headline idea:** "Compare weight-loss programs by what you'll really pay, and how reliably they deliver"
- **Three bullets under it, in buyers' words:**
  - ✓ **One all-in monthly price** at your dose, membership included
  - ✓ **Cancel and refund terms** shown up front
  - ✓ **Ratings from real customers**, not just invited reviews
- **Avoid:** "from $X" prices, raw star ratings as the headline, "#1 rated", before/after photos, results promises.

### B. Each provider card, in this order

| Order | Field | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **All-in monthly price, paying monthly**, with "starts at $X" underneath | Need 6 + fear 1 |
| 2 | **Medication type label**: FDA-approved / compounded injectable / compounded oral | Need 8, compliance |
| 3 | **Reach a human**: phone, chat or text hours | Need 1 |
| 4 | **Cancel online?** Yes / phone only / two subscriptions | Need 5 |
| 5 | **Refill reliability** | Need 4 |
| 6 | **"Best for…" label**: first-timers, switching, insurance, low dose | Stage fit |
| 7 | Button: **"See full price breakdown"** | Pre-qualifies the click |

### C. Modules for each stage
- **Starting out (43%):** a 3-step "How it works" block. Include: a licensed clinician decides, pens vs vials, nausea support, and the cost in month 1 vs month 4. Add a "New to GLP-1s?" quiz route. Tone: no judgment ("most people tried diets for years first").
- **Switching (32%):** a "Switching? Keep your dose" filter and page. Show which providers accept your current prescription, how long until the first shipment, and the price at your current dose compared with LillyDirect and NovoCare.
- **Established (25%):** price at higher and maintenance doses, a pause option, refill reliability, and a "how to cancel" checklist.

### D. Content to publish (each answers a top fear)
1. "What will I really pay? Month-by-month cost by dose" (the true-cost calculator)
2. "Before you prepay: 5 questions to ask about refunds"
3. "How to switch providers without restarting your dose"
4. "Brand-name vs compounded vs compounded pills: what's FDA-approved"
5. "How to cancel [provider]", one page per partner. These are high-intent searches, and showing it protects trust.
6. "How we rate providers": a methodology page explaining the real customer score

### E. Words to use and avoid
- **Use:** real person, felt heard, no judgment, one price, no hidden fees, same price every dose, cancel anytime, ships in 2–3 days, food noise, your journey.
- **Avoid:** "generic Wegovy" or "same as Ozempic" (FDA), "guaranteed approval", body-shaming language, urgency tricks.

### F. How to measure this
- Add the stage question to the quiz and report click-out and partner conversion **by stage**.
- A/B test the hero: drug-led ("Get Wegovy online") vs fear-led ("One all-in price. Cancel anytime. Real people.").
- Track bounce on brand and price ad groups before and after the all-in price column.

## 6. Update after Reddit (11 threads, 102 comments, 63 price reports) — see `11-reddit-voice.md`

**Reddit shows a second buyer.** The profile above is mostly the *Trustpilot buyer*: signing up now, anxious, judging the sign-up and support. Reddit shows the *experienced buyer*: 62% already on treatment, mostly on compounded medication, price-literate to the dollar, and picking by pharmacy. Most experienced buyers started out as Trustpilot buyers and moved after the bill or a refill went wrong.

| | Signing up now (Trustpilot) | Experienced (Reddit) |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | 43% new · 32% switching · 25% established | 62% established · 26% new · 9% switching |
| Compares on | One all-in monthly price, a human who answers | **Price per month at max dose on a 3-month plan**, the **named pharmacy** (503A/503B) |
| Trusts | Reviews, a real person on the phone | Community lists and surveys; **not** Trustpilot ("trustpilot? girl, c'mon") |
| Fears most | Being overcharged, trapped, running out | **Scams** and being overcharged |
| Price they expect | "$39?" (often surprised) | **Compounded about $129** (middle half $100–$165); **brand about $350** ($299–$450) |
| Budget ceiling | Rarely stated | **$100–$200 a month** stated outright |

**Additions to the site recommendations:**
1. Sort by **monthly price at the maintenance dose**. Show both pay-monthly and 3-month price columns, and label 6–12-month prepay as a long commitment.
2. Name the **dispensing pharmacy and its type** (503A / 503B / manufacturer) on every card.
3. Make the first filter **"FDA-approved only / compounded OK / show both"**. People hold firm views on each side.
4. Add **budget chips** ($100 / $150 / $200 / $300+) and a **"No membership"** filter.
5. Add a **5-point safety checklist** to each card: prescriber licensed in your state · named pharmacy · real clinician names · cancel online · refund terms.
6. Add **low starting dose / microdose** and **anti-nausea support** badges for older and side-effect-sensitive buyers. Several commenters are 65–67, and Medicare Bridge is $50.
7. Publish **"Cheapest legal GLP-1 options (and why research peptides are risky)"**. It keeps price-desperate buyers out of the gray market.
8. Publish a **"How we verify"** page with update dates and a buyer survey. The trust standard to beat is the community list "vetted by thousands… ongoing surveys".
9. Make the content **easy for AI assistants to read and cite**: structured tables, schema markup, an llms.txt. Buyers already run provider choices through ChatGPT and Gemini.
