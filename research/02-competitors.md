Assume anything older than about 30 days is stale. Where two sources disagree, I show both.
- Confidence labels used in the tables: **[V]** = consistent across 2+ sources, or from an official or manufacturer source. **[S]** = one secondary source. **[U]** = unverified or conflicting.
- Many "review" sites in this category are themselves affiliates or competitors (formblends, trimrx, trytrimi, therxindex, healingmaps, plexusdx, and PR-wire "awards"). I used them only for price snapshots and flagged them. They are not neutral.

Our comparison page has to choose a lane, or explicitly split the two lanes, because users arrive with very different intents.

## A3. Patterns that win

1. **Branded, weighted methodology made visible.** U.S. News (six pillars with published weights), Forbes (32 factors), and the programmatic sites (6-dimension or 100-point rubrics). The methodology is a selling point and gets its own page (Healthline and MNT "How We Review…").
2. **"Best for [persona]" labels instead of a single ordinal list.** Best Overall, Best for Insurance, Best Without Insurance, Best for Beginners, Best for Returning Patients, Best for Coaching, Best Chat-Based. Users self-select quickly.
3. **Brand-name-only or brand-first framing at the top-tier publishers.** U.S. News excluded compounded-only providers, and Healthline and Forbes lead with WW, Ro, Found, Mochi and Noom. That fits the post-2026 regulatory reality: FDA warning letters to 30 telehealth firms (Feb 2026) and 25 more (June 2026), plus the May 2026 503B proposal.
4. **All-in monthly cost shown (membership + medication)**, as Forbes does for WW Med+. This answers the #1 objection.
5. **Persona or intent hub and spoke**: separate pages for cheapest, semaglutide, tirzepatide, pill, microdose, insurance, and "[brand] vs [brand]".
6. **Visible freshness**: "Updated [date]", "Pricing verified [date]", the year in the title (every top result has "2026").
7. **Third-party proof stacked on top**: U.S. News badge, Trustpilot score and count, "Doctor Reviewed", "Dietitian Approved", "Expert Approved".
8. **Quizzes and matchers with no email gate** (weightlossrankings), and calculators (therxindex).
9. **Affiliate landers lead with a price hook** ("first month $49/$79/$99"), and every card has a CTA.
10. **Regulatory signal in the score**: citedrx and others deduct for FDA warning letters. Consumers increasingly look for legitimacy cues (LegitScript, 503A/503B, FDA-approved).

## A4. Gaps nobody fills well (where we can win)

1. **True 12-month cost, including dose escalation.** Almost every page quotes "from $X". Nobody shows the month-by-month bill as the dose climbs (Wegovy pen $199 intro → $349 → $399 HD; Zepbound vials $299 → $399 → $449; Foundayo $149 → $349; Mochi compounded flat). The complaint data says this is *the* top pain point. **Biggest gap.**
2. **Insurance-first routing.** Only U.S. News scores insurance help, and nobody lets users enter their plan type (commercial / Medicare from mid-2026 / Medicaid / cash) and see which providers actually do prior authorizations, what the realistic copay is ($25 savings cards), and how long the PA takes.
3. **Commitment and cancellation terms in the table.** Found's 6-month commitment and $99 early-cancel fee, WW Med+'s 12-month plan, Ivim's 6-month prepaid bundles, Noom's auto-renew history. Contract terms decide conversion and satisfaction, but only glp1scorecard scores them.
4. **Medication availability matrix as of today**: Wegovy pill, Foundayo, Wegovy HD 7.2 mg, Zepbound vial vs pen, compounded (and its legal status), microdose. Things move monthly, and most pages are stale.
5. **Time-to-prescription and time-to-door.** No major site measures time to Rx or delivery speed, even though Amazon claims same-day delivery and complaints cite late refills.
6. **Brand vs compounded explainer at the decision point**: a short, neutral explanation of safety, regulation, and the price trade-off. Tier 1 sites drop compounded entirely, and tier 2/5 sites hide the risks. Users who choose compounded get no honest guidance.
7. **Direct-from-manufacturer channel comparison.** LillyDirect, NovoCare, TrumpRx and Costco sit alongside the telehealth programs, but are rarely put in the same table ("Do I even need a $149/month membership?").
8. **Review-quality caveats.** Trustpilot is flagged for Henry Meds, and Ivim reports about 40k reviews at 4.9. Nobody normalises ratings or explains them.
9. **Mobile-first, filterable comparison.** The big publishers use long-scroll articles. Nobody offers a fast, sticky, filterable table (needle-free, insurance, budget ceiling, state) *(inferred; layouts not verified)*.

## B3. Notes and cross-provider patterns

1. **Three business models, which users can't easily compare:**
   - **(a) Membership + meds billed separately:** Ro, Hims, WW, LifeMD, Found branded, Mochi, PlushCare, Sesame.
   - **(b) All-in flat price:** Remedy, Orderly, Embody, Eden, SkinnyRx, MEDVi, Fridays, Shed.
   - **(c) Manufacturer direct:** LillyDirect, NovoCare, TrumpRx. You still need a prescriber.
   - A "from $149" in model (a) is often **$298–$600 all-in**, while "$199" in model (b) usually means everything. **Normalising to all-in cost is the single most valuable thing a comparison table can do.**
2. **The price climbs with dose, by design.** Brand self-pay prices are tiered by dose. Wegovy pen goes $199 → $349 → $399 HD; Zepbound $299 → $399 → $449; Foundayo $149 → $349; Wegovy pill $149 → $299. Several compounded players are tiered too (Henry Meds), while others advertise **flat pricing across doses** (Mochi, Eden, Remedy) as a selling point. The bill "roughly doubling by month five" is a documented complaint theme.
3. **Intro-price bait:** first-month offers ($25 WW, $39 Ro/Hims/Mochi, $49/$79 Noom, $79 Embody, $129/$149 Eden, $179 Remedy/MEDVi) followed by much higher recurring prices. This is the #1 trigger for billing complaints.
4. **Commitment and cancellation friction** is the most common complaint category across Hims, Ro, Henry, Found, Noom, Mochi and Remedy. Specific examples: Found 6-month + $99, WW 12-month, Ivim 6-month prepay, Noom quarterly or annual auto-renew.
5. **Trustpilot scores are unreliable as a signal.**
   - The highest scores belong to aggressive compounded marketers (Ivim 4.9 on about 40k, SkinnyRx 4.8, Henry 4.5 *with a fraud flag*, Remedy 4.5, MEDVi 4.3–4.5).
   - The most clinically rated brands score lower (Ro 3.7, Found 3.7–3.8, Hims about 3.9).
   - BBB customer averages run far lower (Noom about 1.2, Calibrate about 1.0).
   - Showing raw Trustpilot stars would systematically favour the riskier providers.
6. **Regulatory risk is concentrated** in compounded-heavy brands: FDA warning letters to MEDVi and Ivim (Feb 2026), and earlier to Hims (Sept 2025). Hims' compounded pill was pulled within weeks, and an OpenLoop breach affected MEDVi's partner.
7. **Oral and needle-free options are now a real choice:**
   - Brand: Wegovy pill and Foundayo, both from $149.
   - Compounded: sublingual, tablet and gum (SkinnyRx, Embody, Henry, MEDVi).
   - "No needles" is a filter users want, but it mixes FDA-approved and unapproved products. It needs labelling.
8. **The insurance moment has arrived.** Medicare covers obesity GLP-1s from mid-2026 ($50 copay cap). Foundayo is $25 with commercial coverage. Amazon One Medical, Ro, Found, Mochi, Calibrate and LifeMD all lead on insurance. An "I have insurance" path probably converts very differently from the cash path.
9. **Juniper is not a US option.** For US readers, "Juniper/Embody" likely means **Embody** (joinem.co). Hims now owns Juniper's parent.
10. **Things I could not verify, to re-check by hand:** Hims' current bundle vs membership pricing; Found's current price card; Fridays' tirzepatide offering; Measured's Trustpilot score and pricing; whether Costco's $499 still exists; and the per-dose pricing on Henry Meds and Orderly.
