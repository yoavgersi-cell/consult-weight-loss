# 05 — Review analysis: Ro (Trustpilot, 138 weight-loss reviews)

**Source:** the user pasted Ro Trustpilot reviews, posted roughly Oct 1–7, 2026. Two erectile dysfunction (sildenafil) reviews were left out, which leaves 138 weight-loss reviews.
**Method:** hand-coded the same way as the Embody set. This paste carries no "Invited" labels, so the reviews can't be split by how they were collected.
**Files:**
- `data/reviews/trustpilot/ro_coded_2026-10-07.csv` — the coded data
- `scripts/review_stats.py` — the script that computes the numbers below

## Headline numbers

- Average **3.87★** across 138 reviews. 63% are 5★ and 25% are 1–2★.
- **66% of 1–2★ reviews are about the membership fee.** People read "$39" as the price and found out afterwards that it's a $39 → $149/month membership with the medication billed on top.
- **81% of 4–5★ reviews are about a quick and easy sign-up**, and 15% say outright "so far so good" or "too soon to tell". Only 4% mention results. Most positive reviews are written at checkout, before any treatment.

## What this means for HealthAccess

1. **This is direct evidence for the all-in price column and the true-cost calculator.** Ro's own reply to almost every complaint is "there are 2 separate charges", with a link to a pricing page. People don't find it before they pay. A comparison page that shows **membership + medication = real monthly cost** answers the exact question these reviewers are asking.
2. **Buyers already use LillyDirect as the price anchor.** One reviewer wrote: *"GO TO LILLY DIRECT AND ONLY PAY THE $299 PER MONTH… That is what they do not want you to know."* Another: *"The price is the same that I pay for at Eli Lilly, except for the membership fees."* The table should show manufacturer-direct prices as a reference row and explain what the membership adds (clinician, coaching, insurance help). Otherwise savvy buyers will work it out themselves and lose trust.
3. **Explaining the price before the click helps partners too.** Buyers who arrive expecting $39 cancel, ask for refunds and leave 1★ reviews. Buyers who arrive knowing the cost is $188 in month one and $298–$448 after that are pre-qualified. That should **raise partner approval and retention rates**, which is a strong point to make to partners and when negotiating payouts.
4. **Ro's real strength is insurance.** Insured reviewers mention approval "the same day" and getting it covered after their own doctor couldn't. The insured path in the router should feature Ro, and the cash path should show its all-in cost honestly.
5. **Positive reviews written at checkout say nothing about the service.** As with Embody's invited reviews, star ratings mostly measure the first step. The real customer score should weight reviews from people at least 30 days into treatment more heavily.
